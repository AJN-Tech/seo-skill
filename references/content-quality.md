# Content quality: helpful, distinct, honest

Search engines reward pages that satisfy a real need. Word count is not a ranking
factor; usefulness, uniqueness and trust are. The audit question is not "is it long
enough?" but "would a customer choose this page over the alternatives?"

## Page purpose test

For every priority page, a reader should be able to answer within one screen:

1. **Who is this for?** A specific audience/segment, not "everyone".
2. **What will I get?** Concrete deliverables or outcomes, not adjectives.
3. **What does setup involve?** Steps, time, dependencies, what you need to provide.
4. **What happens next?** Clear CTA and response expectation.
5. **What does it depend on?** Location, tools, budget, availability — stated honestly.
6. **What proof exists?** Cases, screenshots, numbers the owner can publish. If not,
   leave it out; never invent proof.

## A useful service-page structure

- Intro: the problem and the outcome in plain language.
- Who it is for: segment + situation ("small shops that track sales in notebooks").
- What is included: 3–5 concrete deliverables.
- How setup works: 3 steps with what the client must provide.
- What it depends on: supported tools/markets, limitations, human review.
- Common questions: 2–4 real objections, answered plainly.
- Related services: 2–4 genuinely relevant links.
- Contact CTA with one action.

This is a pattern, not a template to copy verbatim across 37 pages. Each page must
contain content specific to that service; near-identical pages compete with each other.

## Detecting thin and duplicate content

- Main-content word count: below ~150 words is a warning sign, not an automatic fail.
- Near-duplicate detection: shingle the visible text into 5-word sequences; compare
  Jaccard/containment between pages. Above ~0.6 similarity means the pages need
  materially different content, merging, or a canonical strategy.
- Title/description duplication is a strong duplicate signal; fix it first.
- Boilerplate (nav, footer, FAQ shared across pages) should be excluded from comparison.

The bundled auditor reports low word counts and cross-page near-duplicates with a
similarity score and the matching page, so the fix is evidence-based.

## Honesty rules (non-negotiable)

- No invented statistics, prices, timelines, guarantees, certifications, integrations,
  client names, testimonials or logos.
- No "trusted by" claims without a real, permitted customer.
- Do not promise search rankings, revenue, or AI citations.
- Distinguish "we can help you explore" from "we guarantee".
- If the user asks for fake reviews/ratings/schema, refuse the fake data and offer the
  compliant alternative (ask real customers, publish a policy).

## E-E-A-T in practice

- Show who is behind the site: organization name, real contact, location/market.
- Explain process and limits; cite authoritative sources for factual claims.
- Keep content current; remove outdated offers/services.
- Make the site easy to navigate and the business easy to contact.
- Author pages for editorial content; real bios, credentials only if true.
- For regulated topics (health, finance, legal), involve a qualified reviewer and say so.

## AI-assisted content

- AI can draft structure and translations; a human must review facts, tone, and claims.
- Disclose AI assistance where the audience or regulation expects it.
- Do not mass-generate pages that restate the same content for keyword permutations.
- For multilingual sites: review terminology and search intent per market; a literal
  translation of keywords is not localization.

## Content fixes that usually matter most

1. Delete/merge pages with no distinct purpose.
2. Add missing "who / what / how / limits" information to top pages.
3. Replace generic claims with specifics the business can prove.
4. Fix titles/descriptions to match actual page intent.
5. Add internal links from high-authority pages to priority pages.
6. Resolve conflicting offers across pages (same service, different names).

## Review questions for the client

- Which customer conversations repeat most often?
- What do customers ask before buying?
- What can you publish as proof (screenshots, case studies, numbers)?
- Which markets/languages bring real demand?
- What is out of scope, and what should we say instead?
